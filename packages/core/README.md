# @envelope/core

The domain logic of envelope: the budget rules, and later portfolios and rebalancing. It contains only **pure** functions: they take data and return results, and never touch a database, the network or files. That is why they run the same on the server, in the browser and on the phone, and why they are tested in milliseconds.

### Four ideas to take away

1. **Amounts as integer minor units.** `0.1 + 0.2` in JavaScript is `0.30000000000000004`, just like in Perl. With cents (`10 + 20 = 30`) the problem does not exist. Quantities and prices follow the same idea with a fixed scale (× 10^8 and × 10^6), and their products go through `BigInt`, which, like Perl's `Math::BigInt`, is exact at any size.
2. **Pure functions.** `computeBudgetMonth` does not know where its data comes from. The server will read it from PostgreSQL, the phone from SQLite; the rule stays in one place.
3. **Invariant tests.** Besides examples, two tests generate hundreds of random budgets and transaction histories and check that the books always balance: unassigned money + available (payment categories included) + assigned in future + this month's credit overspending = balance of the on-budget cash accounts. They are the safety net for future changes.
4. **No user-facing text.** The core returns data and error codes, never sentences. Words and number formats belong to the UI and depend on the user's language (see [ADR 0004](../../docs/adr/0004-internationalization.md)).

### Converting and rounding

A conversion is computed with `BigInt` on integers only and rounded once, half away from zero, to
the target currency's minor unit (ISO 4217 decimals, `currencyDecimals`):

```
to = from × 10^decimals(to) × rateE10(to) ÷ (rateE10(from) × 10^decimals(from))
```

The euro's `rateE10` is 10^10. So $1,080.00 (108000) at USD 1.0812 is 108000 × 100 × 10^10 ÷
(10812000000 × 100) = 99889.0…, €998.89; ¥10,000 (10000, no decimals) at JPY 162.53 is €61.53; and
£1,000.00 to dollars with GBP 0.8450 and USD 1.0812 is 100000 × 10812000000 ÷ 8450000000 =
127952.6…, $1,279.53, with no rounded GBP→USD rate in between.

A security's value is first its amount in its own currency (`amountOf`, rounded once, ADR 0011),
then converted like any other amount: 3 shares at $33.335 are $100.01, and at USD 2 that is
€50.01 both as the position's line (`valueIn`) and in a total (`convertSum`). A sum is added up per
currency first and each currency's total converted once, so it does not depend on how its amounts
are split.

A total with "Not included" amounts is built by the caller: `convertSum` over the amounts whose
currencies have a rate on or before the date, and every other currency's amounts listed apart, each
summed in its own currency. The converted total is always labelled as leaving those out.

## Current limitations

- Income on a credit card (for example cashback) is not modeled as a transaction here yet: `aggregateTransactions` throws `unsupported_transaction` for an uncategorized transaction on an on-budget card, and for the uncategorized card leg of a transfer with an off-budget account (#598). design.md ("Credit cards") describes the planned rule, an income transaction plus a card payment. Until it is built, the API refuses such a transaction on every write path with `card_income_not_supported` (#548; `apps/api/README.md`, "Income on a credit card stored before #548", has the query that finds any stored earlier). A categorized inflow on a card, such as a merchant refund, is supported.
- Positions do not handle return of capital, amortizing bonds, bonds issued at a discount, spin-offs, mergers, rights issues or dividends paid in shares yet, nor the fiscal view in euros of instruments in other currencies.
- Uncategorized transactions are rejected: the import flow stages rows until every one has a category, is a transfer, or is income (design.md, "Import and reconciliation"). The on-budget leg of a transfer from an off-budget account into a cash account is not uncategorized: with no category it is income to assign (#598).
